# Methods supporting Figure 1A–D

The modelling and species-selection procedures below describe the analysis that generated the supplied inputs. The accompanying code starts from the finalized figure-level tables and reproduces the displayed statistics and figure. Sample-level species profiles and IBS species-level selection evidence are also supplied; the code does not refit the models or rerun the initial species screening.

## Cross-cohort prediction and strictly nested leave-one-dataset-out validation

Species-level relative-abundance profiles from stool shotgun metagenomes were evaluated separately for colorectal adenoma (CRA), colorectal cancer (CRC) and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). For each disease, model performance was assessed by strictly nested leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) validation. In each outer fold, one complete project was held out as the test set. The held-out project was excluded from every model-development step, including prevalence and abundance filtering, univariate feature ranking, correlation filtering, feature-number selection, standardization and model fitting.

Within each training fold, species were retained when detected in at least 5% of samples or at least 10 samples and when their mean relative abundance was at least 1 × 10^-5. Species were ranked using a score combining two-sided Mann–Whitney evidence and absolute log2 fold change: −log10(*p*) × (|log2 fold change| + 10^-6). Candidate features were evaluated in rank order after removing highly correlated species using an absolute Pearson correlation threshold of 0.90 on log10-transformed abundance. The candidate feature number, *K*, ranged from 1 to 200, subject to the number of available features. *K* was selected within each outer training set using an inner LODO procedure across the remaining projects; the value maximizing mean inner validation AUC was selected, with the smaller *K* preferred in the event of a tie.

Relative abundances were transformed as log10(relative abundance + 10^-6). Means and standard deviations used for standardization were estimated from training data only. Classification used logistic regression with inverse-frequency class balancing, the liblinear solver, *C* = 1 and a maximum of 1,000 iterations. Outer-fold discrimination was quantified by the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (ROC-AUC). Stratified bootstrap resampling of cases and controls (5,000 replicates) provided percentile 95% confidence intervals. One-sided empirical *p* values for AUC greater than chance were obtained from 5,000 label permutations with a plus-one correction. Figure 1A shows the project-specific outer-fold estimates; exact values, held-out sample counts and selected *K* are provided in Source Data Figure 1A.

## Descriptive heterogeneity of LODO performance

Figure 1B summarizes the distribution of the outer-fold AUC values for each disease. Points represent individual held-out projects, thin horizontal lines span the minimum to maximum, thick translucent segments show the interquartile range and diamonds mark the median. Cohorts were given equal weight. The IQR is the 75th minus 25th percentile and remains represented by the thick segments in Figure 1B. Mean AUC, sample standard deviation (n−1 denominator), median, quartiles and minimum–maximum range are reported in Source Data Figure 1B. These describe observed cross-cohort performance dispersion, which includes sampling variability as well as cohort composition and model-training differences; they are not formal tests of differences between diseases.

An exploratory supplementary analysis transforms AUC to logit(AUC), approximates the standard error as the transformed 95% CI width divided by 2 × 1.959963984540054, and calculates inverse-variance Cochran Q, I² = 100 × max(0, (Q−(k−1))/Q), and DerSimonian–Laird τ². Raw-AUC-scale estimates are also supplied as a sensitivity comparison. These estimates assume independent cohort estimates and approximately normal errors on the chosen scale. The supplied intervals are bootstrap percentile intervals, and overlapping LODO training sets introduce dependence that is not captured by the available summaries. Consequently these estimates and Q-test p values are exploratory only and are not used for significance claims. Figure 1A annotates the logit-AUC I² estimates beside the disease headings; the figure legend explicitly identifies their exploratory status. The annotated values and assumptions are included in Source_Data_Figure1A_heterogeneity.tsv and the Figure 1A heterogeneity workbook sheet. Full results on both scales are reported in analysis/auc_heterogeneity. A full inferential analysis would require joint covariance and training uncertainty from an appropriate resampling of the full modelling pipeline.

## Selected species sets

The colorectal set comprised the unified Top-300 species ranked from joint CRA/CRC evidence across 10 independent colorectal studies. Within each study, adenoma and CRC samples were combined as the colorectal case group and shared controls were counted once. Study-level evidence was synthesized by signed-Z meta-analysis before ranking. The IBD set comprised its Top-300 eligible ranked species. These are selected species sets; membership does not imply that every selected species meets an individual-species FDR significance threshold.

The IBS comparison was restricted to the Mars_2020 cohort, with 323 case samples and 151 control samples having species profiles. Sample counts refer to profiled specimens and do not establish the number of independent participants. Species identifiers beginning with `s__` were represented as a sample-by-species matrix, with missing sample-species records filled with zero and repeated records summed. A species was retained in the detectable background when `(detection fraction ≥ 0.05 OR detected sample count ≥ 10) AND mean relative abundance ≥ 1 × 10^-5`. Detection meant an input abundance greater than zero, and both detection and mean abundance were calculated across all 474 case and control samples. Abundances were used in their source-export scale without renormalization. This filter yielded 465 background species.

For each of these 465 species, case and control abundances were compared with a two-sided Mann–Whitney test. Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment was applied across the 465 species. Log2 fold change was calculated as `log2((case mean + 10^-6) / (control mean + 10^-6))`. Random-forest screening used five stratified folds. In each fold, a forest was trained on the other four folds, and species with an importance rank of at most 50 were recorded. The selection frequency was the fraction of the five folds in which a species was recorded.

An IBS species was eligible for selection when direction consistency was at least 0.60, it was tested in at least one comparison, and its random-forest Top-50 selection frequency was greater than zero. Direction consistency was the proportion of nonzero study-level fold-change directions matching the majority direction. With only one IBS comparison, this criterion provides no evidence of cross-cohort direction replication. For this single-cohort set, the ranking score can be expressed using the released evidence-table fields as `−log10(bh_q_value) × |log2_fold_change| × direction_consistency × (1 + rf_top50_selection_frequency)`. Up to 300 eligible species were retained. There were only 81 eligible IBS candidates, equal to the union of the five fold-specific Top-50 lists, so all 81 entered Figure 1C–D. No hard threshold on individual-species q value or absolute log2 fold change was applied; the ranking score did not reduce this 81-species set.

The species-level selection evidence for all 465 IBS background species is provided in `input/ibs_species_selection.tsv`. The IBS set represents a single-cohort candidate selection and does not establish that all 81 species are statistically significant differential species. No external IBS species set was included, and the result does not constitute cross-cohort IBS replication. The initial random-forest screening code is outside the scope of the figure-reproduction code supplied here.

## Species-overlap analysis

For each pairwise and three-way comparison, observed overlap was the cardinality of the intersection of the supplied selected-species sets. The primary null model sampled each selected set independently and uniformly without replacement from its own detectable-species background, retaining the observed set sizes. Background sizes were 1,813 species for CRC/CRA, 1,357 for IBD and 465 for IBS (Mars_2020); selected-set sizes were 300, 300 and 81, respectively. Each of 100,000 iterations generated all three random sets and evaluated the same four intersections. No species absent from a group's background could enter that group's random set. The background union was used only to index species identifiers, not as a shared sampling pool.

Expected overlap was the mean simulated intersection count. As a numerical check, the exact expectation was also calculated as the size of the intersection of the participating backgrounds multiplied by the product of each participating group's selected-size/background-size ratio. Empirical upper-tail p values used a plus-one correction, (b + 1)/(B + 1), where b is the number of simulated overlaps at least as large as observed and B = 100,000. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied jointly across the four displayed comparisons. Enrichment required adjusted q < 0.05 and an observed overlap greater than the 95th percentile of the null distribution. These overlap q values test intersections of selected sets, not differential abundance of individual species. The random seed was 20260903. Exact 95% Clopper–Pearson binomial intervals describe Monte Carlo uncertainty in the null tail probabilities; these are not biological-effect or q-value confidence intervals.

This null model controls eligible species availability and selected-set size but assumes uniform selection probabilities within each background. It does not model abundance, prevalence, species correlations or differences in association-selection workflows. Overlap enrichment does not imply concordant association directions or causal sharing.

Figure 1C compares observed overlap with the own-background expectation. Bar heights remain observed shared-species counts; labels above bars report observed/expected fold enrichment (×), and asterisks denote significant enrichment. Figure 1D reports set sizes on the diagonal and pairwise shared-species counts off diagonal. CR in Figures 1C–D denotes the unified CRC/CRA set.

## Figure preparation

The figure was generated with Python using NumPy, pandas, SciPy and Matplotlib. The final canvas is 170 mm × 55 mm. The layout places panel A on the left, B at upper right and C–D at lower right. All figure text uses black Arial Regular, including panel headings, annotations, tick labels and legends. Plotting backgrounds are white with no alternating row shading. PDF and SVG exports retain editable vector objects; the PDF embeds the Arial font. PNG and LZW-compressed TIFF exports were generated at 300 and 600 dpi, respectively.
